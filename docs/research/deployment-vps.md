# Free-tier and cheap VPS / IaaS for an always-on small server

## Summary
Verified against official pages on 2026-09-16. Key 2026 changes: (1) Oracle silently halved the Always Free Ampere A1 allowance on 2026-06-15 to 1,500 OCPU-h + 9,000 GB-h/month (= 2 OCPU / 12 GB, was 4/24) and terminated oversized free-tier instances; the two AMD E2.1.Micro (1 GB) instances, 200 GB block storage and 10 TB egress are unchanged. (2) AWS accounts created on/after 2025-07-15 no longer get the 12-month 750 h EC2 tier; they get up to US$200 credits and the Free plan closes the account after 6 months, so AWS is not a free always-on option. (3) Google e2-micro Always Free still exists but only in us-west1/us-central1/us-east1 (US). (4) Azure still gives 12 months of 750 h/month each of B1s, B2pts v2 (Arm) and B2ats v2 (AMD) Linux VMs if you upgrade to pay-as-you-go within 30 days; after that B1s in Japan East is ~US$9.93/mo compute. (5) Hetzner raised prices twice in 2026 (CX23 now EUR 5.49 + EUR 0.50 IPv4, Germany/Finland only; Singapore has no CX/CAX, cheapest CPX12 EUR 15.49). For East Asia the cheapest reliable paid options with Tokyo datacentres are Vultr vc2-1c-1gb (US$5, Tokyo/Osaka/Seoul/Singapore), Linode Nanode 1 GB (US$5, Tokyo/Osaka/Singapore), ConoHa 1GB (JPY 526/mo on a 12-month ticket, ~US$3.6) and Sakura VPS 1G (JPY 880-990). Tencent Lighthouse (US$4.20, 2 vCPU/2 GB, Tokyo/Seoul) and Alibaba SAS (US$4.50, 1 vCPU/1 GiB, Tokyo) are cheaper per spec but are Chinese-owned providers, which matters for hosting a Gmail refresh token and email contents. Does a server justify itself? A 24x7 VM costs US$0 (Oracle) to ~US$5/mo, which is cheaper than the electricity of leaving a desktop on; the real cost is ops (patching, TLS, auth, backups). If you only need remote access while the PC is on, Tailscale/Cloudflare Tunnel on the PC is enough; if you need Gmail/job-board polling to continue with the PC off, a small VPS is the simplest answer. Every VPS here has a persistent disk, so FastAPI + SQLite + an in-process scheduler runs as-is (the ephemeral-filesystem problem is a PaaS issue, not a VPS one); 1 GB RAM is adequate for this workload (add 1-2 GB swap), 2 GB is comfortable. Always run the app behind a reverse proxy with TLS, restrict access (Tailscale, Cloudflare Access, or at least HTTP basic auth + fail2ban), encrypt the OAuth token at rest, and ship nightly SQLite backups off-box.

Best pick: Oracle Cloud Always Free with home region Japan East (Tokyo) or Japan Central (Osaka): one VM.Standard.A1.Flex sized 1 OCPU / 6 GB (keeps memory utilisation above the 20% idle-reclaim threshold and leaves headroom under the new 2 OCPU / 12 GB cap), 50 GB boot volume, US$0/month, 10 TB egress. Accept that it is best-effort: expect 'out of capacity' retries on a free tenancy, keep nightly SQLite backups off-box, and if it is reclaimed or capacity never appears, fall to the runner-up.
Runner up: Vultr Cloud Compute vc2-1c-1gb in Tokyo (nrt) at US$5/month (1 vCPU, 1 GB, 25 GB SSD, 1 TB) - verified available in Tokyo/Osaka/Seoul/Singapore, flat pricing, no commitment. Equivalent alternative: Linode Nanode 1 GB US$5 in Tokyo/Osaka. If the user is Japan-based and comfortable with a Japanese console, ConoHa 1GB on a 12-month ticket (JPY 526/month, 2 vCPU/1 GB/100 GB, Tokyo) is the cheapest Tokyo option.
